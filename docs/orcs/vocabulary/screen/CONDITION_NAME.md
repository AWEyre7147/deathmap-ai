# CONDITION_NAME

## Technical definition

Name of the agent, cell population, host, genetic background, or culture condition applied (free text curated by ORCS). Multi-valued entries use '|'. '-' means no added condition.

## Plain-language description

The specific thing the cells were exposed to, such as the name of the drug, virus, or immune cell type.

## Where it goes in DeathMap

Screens.condition_name_reported; Screens.interacting_population_reported and Screens.immune_cell_population_reported (when the condition is an immune population)


## Condition categories

Values are split on `|` and each part is assigned one category. Drug and chemical parts also get a mechanism class.

| Category | Technical description | Plain-language description |
|---|---|---|
| `none` | No additional condition; the screen is a baseline time course or untreated selection. | Nothing extra was added. The cells just grew over time, so the screen finds genes needed for normal growth. |
| `genetic_background` | The screened cells carry an additional engineered mutation, knockout, knockdown, or transgene, so the screen measures genetic interactions with that background. | The cells already had one gene changed before the screen. This shows which other genes matter more or less when that first gene is broken. |
| `immune_host_in_vivo` | In vivo screen in which the host mouse strain (immunocompetent vs. immunodeficient) is the selective condition; a comparison between hosts isolates immune-dependent selection. | The cells were grown as tumors in mice. Comparing mice with and without a working immune system shows which genes help tumors survive the immune system. |
| `immune_effector_cells` | Immune effector cells (T cells, NK cells, macrophages, PBMCs, TILs, or CAR T cells) applied as the selective pressure, typically in co-culture with the perturbed population. | Immune cells were added to the cancer cells. Cancer cells that survive point to genes that help them hide from, or resist, the immune attack. |
| `immunotherapy_or_antibody` | An immunotherapy agent or antibody (checkpoint blockade, cancer vaccine, bispecific antibody, therapeutic or stimulating antibody) applied as the selective condition. | A treatment that works through the immune system (like an antibody drug) was used. The screen shows genes that change how well that treatment works. |
| `virus` | Infection with a virus, viral vector, or pseudovirus; selection is usually for host factors required for infection or virus-induced death. | The cells were infected with a virus. Surviving cells point to genes the virus needs to get in or to kill the cell. |
| `bacteria` | Infection or exposure to a bacterial pathogen. | The cells were exposed to bacteria. The screen finds genes the bacteria need, or genes that protect the cell. |
| `microbial_product` | Exposure to a microbial product such as lipopolysaccharide (LPS) that triggers innate immune signaling. | A piece of bacteria (like LPS from the bacterial outer coat) was added to set off the cells' alarm systems. |
| `toxin` | Exposure to a biological toxin; selection usually identifies receptors and trafficking factors required for toxicity. | A natural poison (often from bacteria or plants) was added. Survivors reveal the genes the toxin needs to get in and kill the cell. |
| `cytokine_growth_factor_or_ligand` | Exposure to a cytokine, growth factor, hormone, or purified ligand/protein that engages a cell-surface receptor. | A signaling protein (a message molecule cells use to talk to each other) was added. The screen shows genes that change how cells respond to that message. |
| `cytokine_withdrawal` | Removal of a growth factor or cytokine the cells normally require. | A growth signal the cells usually depend on was taken away. Survivors point to genes that let cells grow without it. |
| `radiation` | Exposure to ionizing or ultraviolet radiation. | The cells were hit with radiation, which damages DNA. The screen finds genes that help cells survive or repair that damage. |
| `culture_condition` | A change in physical or nutritional growth conditions (3D growth, oxygen level, nutrient or medium composition, differentiation, or co-culture with non-immune cells). | The way the cells were grown was changed (for example, in 3D balls instead of flat, or with less oxygen or food). |
| `drug_or_chemical` | A small-molecule drug, chemical agent, or antibody-drug conjugate applied at a defined dose. | A drug or chemical was added. Cells that survive better or worse point to genes that control how cells respond to it. |

## Drug mechanism classes

|---|---|---|

| `mitochondrial_inhibitor` | Mitochondrial electron-transport-chain inhibitor, ATP-synthase inhibitor, uncoupler, or metabolic modulator. | Shuts down the cell's power plants (mitochondria). |
| `ferroptosis_redox_agent` | Ferroptosis inducer (GPX4 or xCT inhibitor), oxidant, or redox-active/antioxidant compound. | Causes (or protects from) chemical stress from reactive oxygen and iron-dependent damage to the cell's fatty membranes. |
| `er_stress_trafficking_cytoskeleton` | ER-stress inducer, secretory-trafficking inhibitor, calcium modulator, lipid signaling agent, or cytoskeletal inhibitor. | Disrupts the cell's protein-shipping system or its internal skeleton. |
| `hormone_receptor_modulator` | Nuclear hormone receptor antagonist or agonist (androgen receptor, retinoic acid receptor). | Blocks or mimics a hormone signal (for example, drugs that block testosterone signals in prostate cancer). |
| `immunomodulator` | Immunosuppressant or immunomodulatory small molecule (calcineurin inhibitor, adenosine A2A agonist, lysosomotropic agent). | A drug that dampens immune-cell activity or changes how cells handle their 'stomach' compartments (lysosomes). |
| `metabolic_inhibitor` | Metabolic pathway inhibitor (cholesterol synthesis, amino-acid depletion, telomerase, G-quadruplex stabilizer, DNA-replication licensing, hedgehog signaling). | Blocks a pathway the cell uses to build or maintain itself. |
| `antibiotic_selection_or_vehicle` | Selection antibiotic, membrane-active antibiotic, vehicle/solvent control, or environmental chemical. | A chemical used as a control or lab tool rather than as a cancer drug. |
| `antiviral` | Antiviral nucleoside analog. | A drug that stops viruses from copying themselves. |
| `antibody_drug_conjugate` | Antibody-drug conjugate: antibody delivering a cytotoxic payload to antigen-positive cells. | An antibody that carries a poison straight to cells with a specific surface marker. |
| `unclassified_compound` | Study-specific or uncommon compound; mechanism not curated here. Verify in the source publication. | A research compound. Check the paper for what it does. |
